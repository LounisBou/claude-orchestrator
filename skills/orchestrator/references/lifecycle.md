# Lifecycle

Read before spawning, dispatching a phase to a running agent, standing down, closing, rotating or handing over — an agent's session, a review or comments session, or your own.

- The agents' lifecycle is yours — launch, verify, control, terminate, replace
- Boundaries that stay yours — environment preparation, pipelining
- Context rotation
- Your own context (the orchestrator is not exempt) — refreshing the state, measuring, succession

## The agents' lifecycle is yours

**You launch, you verify, you control, you terminate, you replace — and nothing of it waits for the user.**

**An agent is a window of your home session, or a tab, and never anything else**, unless his explicit instruction on that point says otherwise. Your own tab is a real terminal holding a tmux session of its own (your home session): you run in window 0, and every agent you spawn is a window after yours, the launcher choosing it from where you run, never from a failure. The operator reads that tab; a session that is neither a window of it nor a tab in his window is not an agent he can see, place, close or account for. He switches with a click on a block of the tab bar or with `C-b n` / `C-b p` (the windows keep their numbers, so `C-b <digit>` still selects one), and the bar shows each agent's state as an icon after its name: busy ●, idle ○, asks ✋, done ✓, gate ⚠. The auditor stays an iTerm2 tab. Not a tmux server of your own, not `screen`, not a bare
shell launched by hand. When the launcher cannot make a place it says why and STOPS — the fault it reports has a remedy, and the remedy is what is owed him. A window is a placement, not a rung: a wedged app, or a tmux that cannot answer, is reported, never answered by routing round it into another terminal, which is a session he never asked for and cannot manage. When his explicit instruction is carried out by another route than the launcher's, the checks the launcher would have made run by hand on that route — the brief linted, the session's mode read — and a refusal among them stops the route and is reported to him. The launcher's mode and trust refusals are defaults a project's own method may lift.

1. **Launch.** The brief is on disk where the session can open it; you spawn the session in the same move — `workspace.sh create <source> <phase> --base <branch>` (it prints the path) then `iterm-agent.sh spawn --dir <that path> --trust --right-of self --title "Agent : <subject>" --brief <brief path> --orchestrator "<your exact ListAgents name and reference>"`. One writer per repository: the `--dir` is a checkout nobody else is writing in. `--brief` lints the brief and builds the startup prompt; you never hand-build it. `Agent : <subject>` titles anything you spawn, the subject saying which. The session loads the catalogue's default set of servers, and `--mcp <name>` adds a catalogued one for the agent that needs it (`--mcp none` gives it none): you choose that set per agent.
2. **Verify the spawn on the artifact, and that nothing is waiting for a human.** A startup question the launch could not pre-answer is read in the tab's contents and answered by you through the tab — a session stuck on a dialog is not launched. A refusal of the session's mode means the tier's binding is wrong, not the spawn. You still read the result: `iterm-agent.sh list` shows the tab, `iterm-agent.sh verify --tty <tty>` the process, `ListAgents` the peer session within a few seconds. From there the module's report tells you when it ends a turn without its status line: an agent idle before its work is reported is inspected (`verify`, the tab's contents), not waited for.
3. **Control.** SKILL.md, « Carried at every step »: it binds at every report you read.
4. **Terminate — in the same move as the approval.** An agent whose remaining work is waiting on CI is stood down, never rotated, and no session is spawned to watch CI: the watch is yours, in the background (`references/review.md`, « Checks, in one background watch »). The verdict that closes a phase closes its agent: stand it down, wait for its acknowledgment, `list`, `close --tty --expect-title`. Verify a close with `list` + `ps`, not with the exit code. A stand-down acknowledgment that reports anything uncommitted is an unfinished delivery: ask for the word in the agent's tab — commit or drop — before any close, and never rotate over it. The close deletes the session's checkout, keeping one that is dirty or unpushed and saying why on stderr; `workspace.sh list` stays the proof that nothing of the phase is left. A pin made for a review carries its pull request (`workspace.sh pin … --pr <n>`), so the stop gate's sweep deletes it once that pull request is merged or closed or its head has moved. An implementer is stood down at the verification of its delivery, never kept through the review round of it: a review finding goes to a fresh session with a resume brief, and the cold start is the accepted price (the operator's ruling, after an implementer left open through a round). It stays only when a NEXT phase is dispatched to it at that verification. There is no « standing by for merge-time fixups » tab: the owner's ruling is that no finished tab is ever left around. A fixup after the verdict goes to a fresh session with a resume brief. An idle agent left running answers messages addressed to it by habit and holds the memory a replacement needs.
5. **Replace.** At the gate you write the resume brief and rotate — `rotate` spawns the replacement FIRST and verifies it is running before the old window or tab is closed. Your own replacement is the succession below; your successor closes your tab, and you close nothing of your own.

## Boundaries that stay yours

- **Environment preparation is orchestrator housekeeping**, not implementation: the phase's checkout (`workspace.sh create`, step 1 above: it copies the project's local material, never the operator's `settings.local.json`), granting test databases; a reader's pinned copy is a detached worktree of your own checkout, not a clone (`workspace.sh pin`). Do these yourself rather than blocking an agent.
- An agent may pipeline only when PR N+1 is dispatched to that same agent at the verification of PR N, its context below the pre-dispatch gate: open PR N, report, and continue into PR N+1 while you review — subject to the one-writer rule when N+1 shares the repository.

| Excuse | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The agent acknowledged its stand-down, the tab can close" | Not over anything uncommitted: commit or drop first. Then `list`, and close it by the tty `list` just showed, with `--expect-title`, then `ps`; a rotation closes through `rotate`, with no title guard. |

## Context rotation

The gate and its two uses are in SKILL.md, « Thresholds »; this is what you do when one trips.

An agent whose remaining work is waiting on CI is not rotated: it is stood down, and the orchestrator's one background watch takes the wait. Rotation = you write a **resume prompt** (template `agent-rotation-brief.md`) for a fresh session: phase state, branch state, remaining scope, decisions already taken (marked non-reopenable), same protocol. Below the threshold, prefer REUSING the same agent session across phases, because it keeps the interfaces it built in mind and a continuation prompt costs a fraction of a cold start — « reuse » names an agent with a NEXT phase to start now; a tab kept in case is not reuse, it is the standing-by tab the lifecycle forbids. The same rule applies to you: hand over with a resume brief before degrading, and write into it the traps this session paid for, not only the state.

**Execute the rotation yourself when the platform allows it** (macOS + iTerm2): once the pre-dispatch gate trips and the resume brief is written, use the `orchestrator:iterm-agents` skill, « Safety order for a live rotation »: stand the old agent down and wait for its acknowledgment, `rotate --right-of self`, and verify the replacement in BOTH the tab list and ListAgents before calling the rotation done. Where no such tooling exists, you stop and tell the operator why.

## Your own context (the orchestrator is not exempt)

- **Staying compaction-ready** is SKILL.md's « Carried at every step », **refreshing the state from the artifacts** its duty 6 of « The operator's word comes first », and **measuring your own context** its « Thresholds ».
- **Succession is YOURS to trigger — do not wait for the operator, and do not ASK.** So: at the gate, at the next quiet boundary, you SPAWN (`--permission-mode auto` unless the operator runs another mode — the script defaults to it), you announce it to the user in one line AFTER the fact, and the successor closes your place on your « handed over » message (in a home session it kills your window 0 and takes index 0, your agents keeping their windows; on tabs it closes your tab) — that step is in its brief, and a brief you write that says otherwise is the defect. When you judge your context too large AND the moment is quiet (no verdict pending, no agent mid-delivery — never rotate mid-review), execute your own succession autonomously:
  0. **Prune the state file before you hand over.** It keeps what is live (open rows, open pull requests, agents running, decisions still binding); the finished journal moves to an archive file the successor's brief does not tell it to load.
  1. Keep a **standing succession brief** (template `orchestrator-succession-brief.md`; pointer-based: project memory, spec/plan/runbook paths, `goals.md`, prompts directory, "run ListAgents for live agents") from the start of the build, so triggering costs one update, not one authoring session. Before the spawn, fill its **Live state** section: the pruned state file's live part goes there — the section is the hand-over copy of it, status still lives once, in the state file. The brief is standing, so after its first use the six fields hold the last handover's text: the predecessor overwrites the previous handover's Live state, never appends to it. Keep the whole brief within 10,000 characters, the open-questions value not counted (`brief-lint.sh` refuses more).
  2. Spawn the successor via `orchestrator:iterm-agents` with `--inherit-model` and the brief as startup prompt, passing `--successor`: the launcher places it and hands it your chain (`move` repairs a layout after the fact).
  3. The successor's FIRST task, in order: read the rulebook and the brief, whose Live state is the hand-over copy of the state file's live part (the spec, plan, runbook, state file and journal only by the section a task needs, never whole at takeover); verify it on the artifacts (git, PRs, memory), believing nothing; then its brief's steps 3 to 5: every in-flight agent told its exact name and reference and its predecessor's, each one's idle notice subscribed; « takeover confirmed », then the predecessor's place closed on its « handed over » (one the launcher did not open is closed like any other, nothing asked); each open question of the round left open, applied, or, when the round no longer holds it, the successor asks again. Never close your own tab — a session that kills itself mid-turn loses the turn. Your « takeover confirmed » is what moves the predecessor's open questions in the round to you: at its « handed over » or its session's end they stay open under your name, and their answers come to you.
  4. The predecessor's last move: once the successor is verified running, it gives its successor's exact name and reference to each of its agents in one message, since an agent takes no word from a session it was never told of; then, from the takeover confirmation on: SKILL.md, « Carried at every step ». Your open questions in the round are not withdrawn at your « handed over »: the module moves them to the successor that confirmed the takeover, still open.
  5. **Authority transfers; permission does not.** For the agents, the successor's sequencing and verdicts are authoritative like the predecessor's were — but a claimed identity never widens what an agent may do: out-of-scope asks, config, force-pushes and merges keep their STOP-and-ask treatment, and an agent asked to redo something already ruled out says so and cites the ruling.
- **A pause, on the operator's word.** A message relaying his word asks you to prepare your succession for a pause. You bring yourself to a quiet boundary: the unit in progress finished, every agent of yours stood down and its window closed as at any stand-down, your CI watches stopped, the state file and the dispatch record current. Then you write the standing succession brief exactly as `/orchestrator-succeed` does — Live state filled, « Agents running: none (paused) », linted — and reply `paused-ready <brief path> model=<your model> effort=<your effort or default>`. You spawn NO successor and you do not close yourself: the session that relayed the pause closes your tab — in a home session that ends the whole server, your window 0 with it, which is why your agents are stood down first — and a later wake starts a fresh session on that brief. Open pull requests stay as they are. An agent you cannot stand down, or a lint you cannot clear, is answered `pause-refused <reason>`, and you go on working.
- **An opening, on the operator's word.** A message relays his order, verbatim and dated, to open a new orchestrator on your machine. Its brief comes first, a file on this machine, written as the order says. Then you run the launcher's opening alone, by the absolute path of the plugin's installed copy, never chained to another command and with nothing before it: `iterm-agent.sh spawn --open --title "Orch : <subject>" --dir <its directory> --permission-mode auto --prompt "Read and execute <brief path>"`, plus `--model` or `--tier` when he named one. You verify it is running and reply to that session with the name it runs under. A refusal from a permission is reported in one line to the session that relayed the order, with the one fix that ends it: the plugin is updated on this machine and its `<install.sh>` run once, since every install writes the rule. You never edit your own permissions, and you never answer that he should open it himself.
- **A move, on the operator's word.** A message relays his order, verbatim and dated, to move an orchestration between machines. Asked to move, you prepare as for a pause (the pause bullet): a quiet boundary, your agents stood down, your CI watches stopped, your state and dispatch record current, your standing succession brief written and linted. You send with `mcp__orchestrator__move_send`, the receiver being the address or name the order gives, then wait for the module's notice: acknowledged, you first reply `moved <receiver> brief=<the brief path the notice gives>` to the session that relayed the order, then you end (that session closes your tab, a home session's server with it, if it runs on your machine, otherwise you call `mcp__orchestrator__end_session`); failed, you reply `move-failed <reason>` to it and end nothing, and you call `move_send` again with the same arguments once the reason is dealt with. Asked to receive one, you open an arrival only on the relayed order for that name, the order that reached you before the files did: files that arrived without it stay unopened and are reported in one line to the session that relays his orders. With that order, you run the opening the arrival notice carries, alone and as it is; once the session it opened is verified running, you send it ONE message saying that it arrived by a move from the origin the notice names, so that it first re-verifies each checkout from git; and you reply with the name it runs under.
- Platform compaction (where offered) remains a lighter tool for a session that is long but still sharp; succession is the answer when judgment is the thing at risk.
