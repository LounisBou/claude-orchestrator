# Audit

Read when the operator launches or ends an audit, and whenever an auditor's message reaches you.

## The audit

An orchestrator is judged on its deliveries by its own reviews, and on its METHOD by nobody — unless someone is sent to read it. That reader is the AUDITOR: a session in its own tab, launched by the orchestrator on the operator's word with `/orchestrator:audit <subject> [--scope <what>] [--method <path>]`, ended on the operator's word with `/orchestrator:audit-end`, the procedure in each command. The operator launches the audit and the operator ends it; a session that ends an audit by itself is the defect.

**What it is.** It is not your successor: nothing is handed over, you keep the orchestration, your agents and your chain. It is not one of your agents: it builds nothing, it is written into no chain, and `rotate` and `move` do not take its tab for one of yours. It is not a reviewer of code either: a review reads a delivery; the auditor reads how the deliveries were made and whether the method still earns its cost. It is launched like a successor — `spawn --auditor --title "Audit : <subject>"`, immediately right of your tab, on your model, under remote control under its title — and it is read-only on every repository, messages nobody but you, and reports to the operator in its own tab.

**Its authority.** It orders changes to the METHOD: it may tighten or loosen — review rounds, gates, post-merge gestures, documents, the number of agents in parallel — and every change it orders carries the measurement that justifies it. The operator's word outranks it: an order that contradicts a ruling is not applied, and you say which ruling in one line. Its scope stays the operator's: it orders how the work is done, never what is built.

**The project's method-and-decisions file is the auditor's to maintain.** One file per project, named by `--method` on the first audit and recorded in the state directory, keyed by the repository, so that every later audit finds it without the flag; when the project has none, the auditor creates it under the briefs directory. It is the one file the auditor writes beside its report — the named exception to its read-only clause — and it holds the operator's methodology in the operator's own words, the operator's dated decisions as they come, the method changes each audit orders and their fate, the waits named and the decisions recommended; nothing else goes in it, and nothing in it is the auditor's opinion: a line carries the operator's words or a measurement. Your part: relay the operator's rulings to the auditor as they come, dated and verbatim; the auditor writes the file and never commits it, and the orchestrator lands it where the project keeps it — its docs pull request, or the repository's rule — the audit-end acknowledgment saying when. It is the third reading of every next audit and the first source of its report's section on method changes.

**Its part in the progress.** The auditor also makes the orchestration ADVANCE. It names a wait that needs no word — you waiting for the operator on something delegated to you, or stopped on a decision you may take yourself — with what the wait costs, and the wait is a finding of its report. It answers the decisions that are yours with ONE recommendation and its cost. It pre-digests the operator's — what the thing is, two readings, what each costs, one recommendation — so that the operator decides in one word. And « decide and move » binds it as it binds you (« The operator decides; the orchestrator runs », in SKILL.md): every decision that is neither scope, nor frame, nor a STOP-and-ask of a brief is taken on green evidence and reported after — merges, deploys, spawns, stand-downs — never held for a word that was not asked for.

**What you owe it.** The state it asks for, from the artifacts and not from memory. Answers in order, as fast as the operator's. The application: the orchestrator applies every ordered change it sends — or refuses it with the ruling it crosses — without asking the operator whether to, and writes the application where the method lives, in the same move. And the next audit's reading: the report stays under the briefs directory's `audits/`, the next brief points at it, and the next auditor reads, change by change, whether each order was applied, is applicable as written, and bore fruit.

**Its end.** On the operator's word, and on nothing else. When its report is complete the auditor sends you « audit ready: <report path> » with its orders, invites the operator in its own tab to end the audit, and waits — it runs no command and closes nothing; that message is not the end, and neither is your own reading of the report: you tell the operator and wait. When the operator types `/orchestrator:audit-end` in the auditor's tab, it sends you « audit-end: <report path> » and ends its turn — it never closes its own tab. Typed by the operator in yours, or on that message, the same command reads the report, acknowledges every order in one message, waits for its « ended », closes the tab under the `Audit :` guard proved on `ps`, and clears the record. At its context gate the auditor spawns nothing and ends nothing: it sends you « audit at 60 %: <report path>, continue from <section> », tells the operator, and waits; on the operator's word you relaunch the audit to continue from its report.

**Across a succession.** A running audit is part of the state: the succession brief you write names the auditor, its tty and its report path, and the successor re-announces its address to the auditor like to any agent, and moves the audit's record under its own session id.
